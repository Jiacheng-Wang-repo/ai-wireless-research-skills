# Reviewer Response Writing

Build on the reusable scientific-writing baseline rather than duplicating it.
Read [academic-writing](../academic-writing/SKILL.md) completely before drafting
or auditing a response. Its manuscript guidance against imagined objections
does not remove the obligation to answer a reviewer's actual concern. Retain
the technical explanation, evidence and reasoned rebuttal needed for complete
coverage; this skill owns response stance and truthful completion wording.

## Establish Authority

Treat these roles as cumulative and non-competing:

1. Repository and workflow policy governs repository behavior.
2. The Academic Writing Skill provides reusable scientific-writing discipline.
3. This Reviewer Response Writing Skill adds response-specific discipline.
4. Paper-, venue-, and revision-specific constraints add stricter local
   requirements where applicable.
5. The canonical manuscript, response, and supporting evidence determine
   factual truth and actual submitted or current content.

Apply all applicable roles together. Neither reusable skill can override
paper-specific facts, canonical wording, evidence, or user-confirmed decisions.

## Response Workflow

### 1. Identify the Actual Concern

- Preserve the reviewer or editor label, comment number, attribution, and
  comment text as historical source material.
- Determine what is being requested: explanation, correction, evidence,
  comparison, experiment, limitation, or manuscript change.
- Separate the valid concern from any unsupported or incorrect premise. Address
  the concern professionally without silently accepting the premise.
- If the comment contains several concerns, answer each one explicitly while
  preserving the original correspondence.

### 2. Classify the Response Action

Classify the work before describing it:

- **Clarification only:** Explain existing method, evidence, or wording; no
  manuscript change.
- **Manuscript wording revision:** Change prose, notation, caption, table, or
  presentation without changing the underlying study.
- **Additional analysis:** Analyze existing data or evidence without collecting
  a new experimental result.
- **New experiment:** Generate and validate new empirical evidence.
- **Response-only diagnostic:** Provide limited evidence to address the comment
  without promoting it into the manuscript's main evidence package.
- **Limitation or future work:** State what remains outside the completed scope.

Use multiple classifications when necessary. Do not describe one category as
another; in particular, do not call a clarification a new validation or an
exploratory diagnostic a completed manuscript experiment.

### 3. Verify Current State

- Check the current manuscript, response, evidence, and completion status before
  drafting.
- Never claim that an experiment, analysis, revision, validation, or result was
  completed when it was only proposed, planned, partial, or unavailable.
- Do not infer completion or failure from a stale draft or missing local
  artifact. Report uncertainty and verify the authoritative source.
- Do not promise an experiment or revision unless it is authorized and will be
  performed. Distinguish a proposal from completed work.

### 4. Draft the Direct Answer First

- Open with a concise professional acknowledgment when appropriate, then answer
  the concern directly before adding background.
- State the technical position accurately: agree, partially agree, clarify, or
  respectfully correct the premise as warranted.
- Avoid defensive, argumentative, dismissive, or overly apologetic phrasing.
  Acknowledge a valid clarity or evidence concern without overstating fault.
- Explain the action taken, where it appears, and why it addresses the concern.
- Provide enough method or evidence detail for a substantive comment, but omit
  background that does not help resolve it.

### 5. Describe Evidence and Revisions Precisely

- Distinguish what the evidence directly demonstrates from interpretation,
  limitation, and future work.
- Keep every response claim aligned with the current manuscript and supporting
  evidence.
- Do not promote smoke tests, exploratory analyses, diagnostics, or
  response-only experiments into manuscript-level conclusions without explicit
  scientific and editorial justification.
- Use the smallest scientifically sufficient manuscript revision. Preserve
  unrelated argument structure and already approved wording.
- Explain relevant logic in response prose rather than relying only on a figure
  caption, table note, or attachment.

### 6. Quote Revised Manuscript Text Selectively

- Quote revised text when it materially demonstrates how a substantive concern
  was resolved; do not require a quotation for every minor edit.
- If response text is presented as a quotation from the manuscript, reproduce
  the current canonical manuscript wording verbatim. Allow formatting-only
  adaptation when necessary, but do not alter technical wording, equations,
  numerical values, qualifications, citations, or scientific meaning.
- Do not present a paraphrase as a manuscript quotation.
- Identify the revision location precisely enough for verification.
- Treat reviewer/editor quotations, rejected drafts, and historical text as
  source records, not as approved author wording.

### 7. Handle Incomplete Resolution Honestly

- State a remaining limitation, evidence gap, or scope boundary directly when
  the concern cannot be fully resolved.
- Explain what was clarified or improved despite the remaining limitation.
- Do not fabricate closure, mark an unresolved item complete, or imply reviewer
  agreement that has not occurred.
- Present future work as future work, not as evidence supporting the current
  paper.

### 8. Check Structure and Correspondence

- Preserve comment numbering, reviewer/editor attribution, and one-to-one
  comment/response mapping.
- Keep multi-part answers in the same order as the concerns unless a clearer
  mapping is explicitly provided.
- Make each response sufficiently self-contained to be reviewed independently.
- Keep the response concise enough to scan while retaining the technical
  evidence needed to close the comment.

## Flexible Response Pattern

Use this pattern when it improves clarity; do not force every minor response
into all parts:

1. **Direct answer:** Acknowledge and answer the concern.
2. **Action and evidence:** State the action classification and supporting
   evidence.
3. **Manuscript change:** Identify what changed, where, and why.
4. **Revised text:** Quote the current manuscript selectively.
5. **Boundary:** State any unresolved limitation or future work honestly.

## Final Response Check

Before handoff, verify:

1. The response answers the actual concern rather than a nearby topic.
2. Every claimed action or result is complete and evidence-supported.
3. Reviewer premises are handled accurately rather than accepted by default.
4. Response claims, manuscript claims, quotations, and evidence agree.
5. Diagnostics and response-only evidence retain their intended scope.
6. The manuscript revision is no broader than scientifically necessary.
7. Limitations and future work are not presented as completed evidence.
8. Tone is professional, direct, and non-defensive.
9. Numbering, attribution, and comment/response correspondence are preserved.

## Exclusions

Do not use this skill to supply:

- general scientific claim calibration already governed by the Academic
  Writing Skill;
- paper-specific methods, terminology, results, reviewer decisions, or evidence
  boundaries;
- venue-specific formatting not established by the applicable instructions;
- Git, worktree, environment, file-routing, artifact-protection, or publication
  synchronization policy;
- numerical values, configurations, seeds, hardware facts, or provenance that
  must come from canonical artifacts and evidence records.
